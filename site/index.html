The Feel dial

From the grid to the pocket.

Most humanize buttons shake every hit at random, and the beat just sounds sloppy. Feel plays it like a drummer instead.

Turn one dial from Machine, dead on the grid, to Drunk. Presets in between: Studio tight, In the pocket, Laid back, Live drummer, Rushing and Loose.

Every instrument plays its own part of the pocket. Kicks stay tightest. Snares and claps sit back or push. Hats breathe the most. Timing drifts smoothly from hit to hit, the way a player’s hands do, and Dynamics leans on the downbeats.

The same setting plays the same take every loop, so the groove stays put. Press New take for another performance, or let every pass vary. Each channel has its own amount, so you can keep the kick robotic while the hats swing.

What’s in the kit

Steps, songs, sounds.

If you’ve made beats in a pattern-based studio before, you’ll know your way around in a minute.

Program

A channel rack of steps

One row per sound, 1 to 4 bars of steps. Click to add a hit, drag to paint a roll, right-click to clear. Swing, mute, solo, pan and volume on every row.

Arrange

Patterns on a playlist

Make as many patterns as you want and lay them out on twelve tracks. Stretch a clip and it repeats its pattern. Play one pattern or the whole song.

Structure

Intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro

Mark your song’s sections over the playlist. Loop any section while you work on it, duplicate it with its clips, or export just that part.

Sounds

Five kits, 808s, 14 synths

Dark Roast, Espresso, Cold Brew, Drip Machine and Night Shift kits, three 808s that play in key, and synths from sub bass to pads, keys and bells.

Make your own

Record a sample

Hit record and clap, sing or bang a pot lid. Silence is trimmed and the level set, then it drops into the rack. Reverse it, loop it, or fit it to your tempo.

Finish

MP3 and WAV, saved projects

Export the song, one pattern or one section as MP3 or 24-bit WAV. Save the whole project, your own samples included, as one .edk file.

Questions

Asked and answered.

Is it really free?

Yes. No paid tiers, no ads, no account, no trial and no data collection. The source code is public under the GNU GPL, so anyone can read it, change it and share it.

Does it track me?

No. The app has no analytics and never sends anything over the internet. Your beats, samples and settings stay on your device.

Can I sell beats I make with it?

Yes. What you make is yours. Every factory drum sound is synthesised by the app itself, so there are no sample licences to worry about either.

Will it open in Safari or on an iPhone?

Yes, from this site: use the “Open it in your browser” button. Safari won’t start the audio engine from a file you open directly, so if you download the web version, run its Start launcher instead.

Does it load VST or AU plugins?

Not yet. Plugin hosting is planned for the desktop versions. Phones and browsers can’t load outside plugins, so everything built in works everywhere.

How is it related to DAWters and Son?

It’s the beat-making half of the same suite. Both share the sound engine and the look, and both are free and open source.

How do I report a bug?

Open an issue on the project’s code page with what you did, what you expected and what happened, plus your device and the version from File › About.
